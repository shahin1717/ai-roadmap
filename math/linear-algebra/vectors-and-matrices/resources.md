# Resources: Vectors and Matrices

Titles, authors, chapter pointers, and free links only. Items marked *(library)* are good books that are not free to download; borrow or buy them if you can.

## Read

| Source | Where to look | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| Deisenroth, Faisal & Ong, *Mathematics for Machine Learning* ([free PDF](https://mml-book.com)) | Ch. 2.2 (Matrices); Ch. 3.1-3.4 (Norms, Inner Products, Lengths and Distances, Angles and Orthogonality) | Matrices and products; length, dot product, angle, written with ML in mind |
| Strang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra*, 5th ed. *(library)* | Ch. 1.1 (Vectors and Linear Combinations); Ch. 1.2 (Lengths and Dot Products); Ch. 1.3 (Matrices); Ch. 2.4 (Rules for Matrix Operations) | The classic, picture-first treatment of everything on this page |
| Strang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra*, 6th ed. *(library)* | Ch. 1.1 (Vectors and Linear Combinations); Ch. 1.2 (Lengths and Angles from Dot Products); Ch. 1.3 (Matrices and Their Column Spaces); Ch. 1.4 (Matrix Multiplication AB and CR) | Newer edition, same ideas with a stronger focus on columns |
| Strang, *Linear Algebra and Learning from Data* *(library)* | Part I, Sections I.1 (Multiplication Ax Using Columns of A) and I.2 (Matrix-Matrix Multiplication AB) | The column view of matrix multiplication, aimed at machine learning |
| Aggarwal, *Linear Algebra and Optimization for Machine Learning* *(library)* | Ch. 1.2.1 (Basic Operations with Scalars and Vectors); Ch. 1.2.2 (Basic Operations with Vectors and Matrices); Ch. 1.3 (Matrix Multiplication as a Decomposable Operator) | Operations with a machine-learning flavor and many worked examples |

## Watch

- 3Blue1Brown, *Essence of Linear Algebra*, chapters 1-4 (vectors, linear combinations, linear transformations, matrix multiplication as composition) and chapter 9 (dot products): <https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/linear-algebra>
- MIT OpenCourseWare, *18.06 Linear Algebra* (Gilbert Strang), Lecture 1 (The Geometry of Linear Equations) and Lecture 3 (Multiplication and Inverse Matrices): <https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-06-linear-algebra-spring-2010/>

## Try

- NumPy, *Absolute Beginners* guide, for arrays and the `@` operator: <https://numpy.org/doc/stable/user/absolute_beginners.html>
- Matplotlib, *Quick start guide*, for drawing arrows and plots: <https://matplotlib.org/stable/users/explain/quick_start.html>
